# **HINDRANCES**

**Hindrances in Tangent are character flaws, quirks, or hindrances that provide additional Character Points (CP) during character creation. These can add depth, realism, and role-playing opportunities to a character, but they also come with drawbacks or limitations.**  

**Types of Hindrances**  
Hindrances can be physical, mental, social, or a combination of these. Examples include phobias, addictions, chronic illnesses, physical disabilities, or even personality traits like impulsiveness or arrogance.

**CP Value**  
Each Hindrance has a CP value assigned to it, reflecting the severity of the drawback it imposes. More significant flaws grant more CP, allowing players to invest in other areas of their character.

**Balancing Act**  
While Hindrances offer a way to gain extra CP, players should be mindful not to overload their characters with too many flaws. A character with numerous severe hindrances might become difficult or unfun to play. The recommended limit is no more than 15 points of Hindrances.

**Role-Playing Opportunities**  
Hindrances can create interesting role-playing opportunities and challenges. Players can explore how their character's flaws affect their interactions with others, their decision-making, and their overall approach to the game world.

**Architect Involvement**  
The Architect plays a crucial role in approving Hindrances and ensuring they are appropriate for the setting and the character's concept. They may also offer suggestions or work with the player to develop unique Hindrances that fit the character's background and personality.

**Example**  
A character with a fear of heights (Acrophobia) might gain 6 CP, reflecting the significant impact this phobia can have on their actions and choices. This could lead to interesting role-playing moments, such as the character hesitating to climb a tall tower or panicking when crossing a narrow bridge.

Hindrances are an optional but valuable tool for character creation in Tangent. They enable players to create more complex, nuanced, and memorable characters, and they can enrich the role-playing experience by introducing challenges and opportunities for growth.

# **HINDRANCES COMPENDIUM**

## 

## **System Overview**

In the Tangent Role-Playing System, **Hindrances** are negative traits, flaws, or hindrances that a character possesses. Unlike standard features which cost Character Points (CP), Hindrances **grant** additional CP during character creation. This allows players to "buy" more positive traits, skills, or attribute scores by accepting specific limitations.

### 

### **Design Philosophy**

**Depth & Realism:** Hindrances are designed to flesh out a character's personality and history. A character with a phobia, a debt, or a missing limb is often more narrative-rich than a "perfect" character.

**Role-Playing Incentives:** These flaws provide hooks for the Architect to create complications, driving the story forward.

**The Balancing Act:** While tempting for the extra points, players are cautioned against taking too many. A character crippled by flaws may become unplayable or a burden to the party.

### 

### **Mechanics & Limits**

**Character Points (CP):** Each Hindrance has a specific CP value (e.g., 2, 3, 6\) based on its severity. These points are added to the character's starting pool.

**Recommended Cap:** The standard limit for Hindrances is **15 CP**. Exceeding this requires Architect approval and usually indicates a highly flawed or tragic character concept.

**Architect Discretion:** The Architect has the final say on whether a Hindrance is valid for the campaign setting. A "Fear of Water" Hindrance is free points in a desert campaign and may be disallowed or reduced in value.

## 

## **Terminology Distinction**

**Important:** Do not confuse **Hindrances (Character Flaws)** with the **Rolling with Hindrance** mechanic.  
**Hindrances (Capital D):** Permanent or semi-permanent character traits chosen at creation (e.g., "Blindness," "Debt").  
**Rolling with Hindrance (lowercase d):** A situational mechanic where a player must roll 2d10 twice and take the **lowest** result (Disadvantage).  
*Note:* Some Character Hindrances (like *Impaired Sense*) may cause a player to *Roll with Hindrance* on specific checks.

## 

## **Categories of Hindrances**

Hindrances are grouped into seven primary categories to help define the source of the trouble.

### **I. Background Hindrances**

*Relating to the character's past history, upbringing, or previous entanglements.*  
**Examples:** Dark Secret, Wanted by the Law (Mundane), Non-Monetary Debt, Sworn Enemy, Traumatic Past (Triggers), Unwanted Legacy.

### 

### **II. Mental Hindrances**

*Relating to cognitive function, memory, or intellectual barriers.*  
**Examples:** Poor Memory, Learning Disability, Arrogance, Paranoia, Impaired Reasoning, Low Self-Esteem.

### 

### **III. Metaphysical Hindrances**

*Relating* to magic, psionics, the soul, or supernatural *forces.*  
**Examples:** Curses, Divine Disfavor, Haunted, Magical Vulnerability (e.g., Cold Iron), Geas/Pact restrictions, "Weirdness Magnet."

### 

### **IV. Physical Hindrances**

*Relating to the body, health, and sensory capabilities.*  
**Examples:** Missing Limbs, Blindness, Deafness, Chronic Illness, Frailty, Low Pain Tolerance, Allergies, Physical Deformities.

### 

### **V. Psychological Hindrances**

*Relating* to behavior *patterns, compulsions, and personality restrictions.*  
**Examples:** Phobias, Addictions, Obsessions, Strict Codes of Honor, Vows of Pacifism/Silence, Impulsiveness, Greed, Laziness.

### 

### **VI. Resource Hindrances**

*Relating to assets, wealth, and equipment.*  
**Examples:** Poverty, Massive Financial Debt, Lack of Shelter, Dependency on Rare Substances (Maintenance).

### 

### **VII. Social Hindrances**

*Relating to standing in society, reputation, and relationships.*  
**Examples:** Bad Reputation, Minority/Xeno Stigma, Socially Awkward, Dependent (NPC), Debt of Honor.
