# 1.01.04 ATTRIBUTES & SAVING THROWS

* **Focus Statement:** The complete mechanical and narrative reference for the six Primary Attributes (**Strength**, **Agility**, **Stamina**, **Intellect**, **Wisdom**, **Charisma**), straight `+5 CP / -5 CP` pricing, General Attribute Checks, Active and Passive Saving Throws (`2d10 + 2 + [2 * Attr]` / `CR 12 + [2 * Attr]`), and the `+1 per 5 over CR 10` Skill Synergy protocol.

## Overview & Mechanical Authority Block

Every rule, formula, and character creation procedure in **Volume I: The Operator's Manual** (`operator/`) is governed by these canonical mechanical invariants:

* **Core Resolution Roll:** `2d10 + Skill Rank + Attribute Modifier + Situational Modifiers vs. Challenge Rating (CR)`.
* **Advantage & Disadvantage:** Roll `+1d10` per stack of Advantage (keep the highest 2 dice) or Disadvantage (keep the lowest 2 dice). Opposing stacks cancel 1-for-1.
* **Critical Hit & Fumble Resolution:**
  * **Critical Hit (`Natural 20` / double-10s on `2d10`, or expanded threat range `19`, `18`):** `2x Base Weapon Damage Dice + 30 to Attack Score + Weapon/Elemental Critical Effect`.
  * **Fumble (`Natural 2` / double-1s on `2d10`, or expanded fumble range `3`, `4`):** `-10 to Attack/Check Score + Situational Complication`.
* **Character Point (CP) Economy (`150 CP` Starting Budget):** Starting Operators receive **150 Character Points (CP)**, structured as an **80-CP Archetype Chassis** plus a **70-CP Customization Budget** (or allocated via full custom point-buy):
  * **Primary Attribute at `+3`:** `15 CP` (`+5 CP` per `+1`)
  * **Secondary Attribute at `+2`:** `10 CP` (`+5 CP` per `+1`)
  * **4 Essential Skills at Rank 6 (Trained):** `24 CP` (`1 CP` per Rank)
  * **6 Supporting Skills at Rank 3 (Novice):** `18 CP` (`1 CP` per Rank)
  * **2 Signature Traits (`1 CP` each) + 3 Recommended Core Features (`9 CP`):** `13 CP`
  * **Customization Budget:** `70 CP`
* **Background Allocations (`60 SP` at `0 CP`):** `20 SP Faction + 20 SP Origin + 20 SP Occupation = 60 Skill Ranks`, `4 Recommended Features`, and `4 Traits` at `0 CP` (`Extra Traits = 1 CP` each).
* **Universal Straight Pricing:** `+5 CP` per `+1` Attribute, `-5 CP` refund per `-1` Attribute penalty, and `1 CP` per `1` Skill Rank (`5 CP` per `5` Skill Ranks) across both Species Packages and post-species customization (zero package discount).
* **Vitality, Health & Structure Pools (Zero Stamina Scaling):** Biological characters start with **Base 30 Vitality** and **Base 30 Health**; Synthetic/Construct characters combine both into **Base 60 Structure Points (SP)**. Pools scale strictly at **`+5 points per 1 CP`** spent (Suggested Max: `60` each / `120 SP`; **NEVER scaled by Stamina**). The **Stamina Modifier** serves as **Base Toughness**, subtracting point-for-point from incoming wound damage after Armor DR.
* **Personal Hindrance Cap (`+10 CP` Maximum):** Personal character Hindrances grant at most **`+10 CP`** during creation. **Species-level hindrances and cybernetic/xeno Stigmas are exempt from this 10-point cap.**

## Part II — Complete Unabridged Profiles & Rules Entries

### 1. Cost, Starting Maximum & Tier Benchmarks

Attributes are a crucial aspect of character creation and gameplay. They represent a character's natural aptitudes and talents, influencing their success in various tasks and challenges. The six core attributes are **Strength (`STR`)**, **Agility (`AGI`)**, **Stamina (`STA`)**, **Intellect (`INT`)**, **Wisdom (`WIS`)**, and **Charisma (`CHA`)**. Each score modifies skill checks that use the associated attribute, providing bonuses or penalties based on the character's strengths or weaknesses. Additionally, attribute checks are used as a fallback for actions not covered by specific skills and as saving throws against harmful effects.

* **Cost and Starting Maximum (`+5 CP` per `+1` / `-5 CP` per `-1`):** Both inside a **Species Package** (`1.03 SPECIES.md`) and during post-species customization, for every `+1` point increase in an attribute, the cost is a straight **5 Character Points (CP)** (and every `-1` penalty refunds **-5 CP**, with zero package discount). This cost reflects the significant impact that even a small improvement in an attribute can have on a character's capabilities. For example, a character with a Strength of `+2` can carry more weight and deal more damage in melee combat than a character with a Strength of `+1`. Similarly, a character with a Charisma of `+3` is more likely to persuade others and negotiate favorable outcomes than a character with a Charisma of `+2`.
* **Balanced Starting Ceiling (`+4` Raw):** The suggested maximum starting attributes of **+4**, adjusted by Species modifiers, provides a balanced approach to character creation. This ensures that characters are not overly powerful at the start of the game, while still allowing players to create characters with unique strengths and weaknesses. By carefully allocating their CP, players can create characters that excel in specific areas while having room for growth and development as the game progresses. Whether they choose to focus on physical prowess, mental acuity, or social influence, the investment in attributes is a crucial step in bringing a character to life.
* **Paragon Ceiling (`5 + Species Modifier`):** During Character Creation, no Attribute may start higher than **+4** without Species modifiers or purchased Augmentations. The natural upper tier of any Attribute is **5 plus the species modifier**, indicating a biological paragon of their people.
* **Attributes Modify Skill Checks:** Each point in a character's attributes provides a bonus (or penalty) to Skill Checks that use the associated attribute. For example, a character with an Agility score of `+2` has a `+2` bonus to Skill Checks that use Agility, such as Attack rolls for ranged weapons.

#### A. Average Score (`+0`)

The average score for each attribute is **zero (`+0`)**, representing an average human with no particular strengths or weaknesses. This means that the individual possesses a balanced set of skills and abilities, without any significant advantages or disadvantages in any specific area. They are neither exceptionally gifted nor noticeably lacking in any particular domain.

This average human has a moderate level of physical strength, agility, and endurance, allowing them to perform everyday tasks and activities without difficulty. However, they may not excel in physically demanding or athletic endeavors compared to individuals with higher scores in these Abilities.

Regarding mental capabilities, the average human has a reasonable level of intelligence, memory, and problem-solving skills. They can effectively learn new information, analyze situations, and make informed decisions. Yet, they might not possess the exceptional intellectual brilliance or creative genius of individuals with higher scores in these Abilities.

In terms of social skills, the average human has a decent level of empathy, communication, and interpersonal relationships. They can interact with others effectively, build relationships, and work collaboratively. However, they may not be as charismatic or influential as individuals with higher scores in these Abilities.

It is important to note that being average does not imply mediocrity or lack of potential. The average human is capable of achieving great things through hard work, dedication, and continuous self-improvement. They can develop their skills and abilities over time, potentially surpassing those with initially higher scores through consistent effort and practice.

#### B. Exceptional Score (`+5` and Above)

An exceptional attribute grants significant bonuses to related skills and checks, empowering the individual to excel in their chosen endeavors.

With a score of **+5** (or `5 + Species Modifier`), an individual gains a profound understanding and mastery of their attribute. They can effortlessly perform complex tasks and overcome challenging obstacles that would prove daunting to others. Their enhanced perception and coordination allow them to make split-second decisions and execute intricate maneuvers with precision and grace.

Furthermore, individuals with an exceptional attribute often inspire awe and admiration in others. Their prowess and skill captivate onlookers, leaving them in awe of the seemingly impossible feats they can accomplish. This recognition can open doors to opportunities and collaborations, enabling the individual to make a lasting impact on the galaxy at large.

#### C. Non-Attribute (`-25 CP` Structural Flaw)

A character or construct with a **Non-Attribute (`—`)** score in a particular attribute is severely limited in their capabilities. They cannot perform actions that rely on that attribute, and they **automatically fail** all checks associated with it. This is represented as a **-25 CP flaw** in character/chassis creation.
* *Examples:* Examples of Non-Attribute entities include a stationary Intellect Construct core lacking both **Strength** and **Agility**, or a Simple Automata Mecha running on basic deterministic programming but lacking any actual **Intellect, Wisdom, or Charisma** (not a standard PC, but an architectural/construct example).

### 2. Attribute Checks & Saving Throws

Attribute checks are a fallback for actions not covered by specific skills. They measure a character's raw capabilities and are used in general checks, saving throws, and optionally as default untrained rolls. Attribute checks encompass a wide range of situations and are categorized into two types: **General Checks** and **Saving Throws**.

> **CRITICAL ADJUDICATION RULE:** Attribute Checks (and their `2 + [2 * Attribute]` Save bonuses) are **NOT** to replace actual Skill Checks and are to be used as Saves or raw attribute tests—never as a workaround for not having a skill. If a roll is covered by a Skill which the character lacks, the default untrained roll is simply `2d10 + Raw Attribute Score + Modifiers` (with any applicable untrained penalty)—**NOT** an Attribute Save Check.

#### A. General Checks vs. Saving Throws

* **General Checks:** General attribute checks are employed when a character attempts an action not directly associated with a specific skill. These checks assess a character's natural abilities and raw proficiency in a particular area. For instance, a character may make a **Strength (Might)** attribute check to break down a jammed door or an **Agility (Reflex)** attribute check to catch a falling object.
* **Saving Throws:** Saving throws represent a character's ability to resist harmful effects, such as invocations, toxins, traps, or environmental hazards. Each saving throw is associated with a specific attribute score, and the outcome determines whether the character succeeds in negating or mitigating the effect. For example, a character makes a **Stamina (Fortitude)** saving throw to resist poison or a **Wisdom (Willpower)** saving throw to avoid being charmed.
* **Difficulty & Versatility:** The Challenge Rating (`CR`) of the check is determined by the Architect based on factors such as the task's complexity and the hazard's tier. The result of the check determines whether the character succeeds, fails, or critically succeeds (`Natural 20`) or fumbles (`Natural 2`) in the action. Attribute checks add depth and versatility to Tangent gameplay, allowing characters to navigate a wide range of challenges using their innate capabilities.

#### B. How Attribute Checks & Saving Throws Are Made

1. **Determine the Relevant Attribute:** Identify which Attribute Score corresponds to the action or resistance roll being attempted.
2. **Calculate the Base Score:** The base score for an Attribute Check / Active Saving Throw is `2 + (Attribute Score * 2)`:
   ```text
   Active Save Roll = 2d10 + 2 + (2 * Attribute Score) + Feature Bonus + Situational Modifiers vs. CR
   Passive Save CR  = 12 + (2 * Attribute Score) + Feature Bonus
   ```
3. **Apply Modifiers:** The Architect may apply modifiers based on the difficulty of the task, environmental factors, gear, or other relevant circumstances.
4. **Roll the Dice:** Roll **2d10** and add the result to the modified base score.
5. **Compare to the Challenge Rating (`CR`):** If the total equals or exceeds the **Challenge Rating (CR)** set by the Architect, the check is successful.

* **Additional Notes — Attribute Score Improvements & Independent Save Cost:**
  * **Attribute Score Improvements:** As a character's attributes increase, their Attribute Checks/Saves automatically become more effective, gaining a **+2 point increase** from the recalculation (`2 * Attribute`).
  * **Independent Save Cost (`1 CP` per `+1`):** Increasing the score of an Attribute Check/Save separately costs **1 Character Point (CP) per +1 point**, without needing to improve the associated Attribute Score.

#### C. Types of Challenges

1. **Targeted Challenge (vs. Static `CR`):**
   This is a type of Attribute Check where success or failure is determined by comparing the character's `2d10` roll to a target number set by the Architect, called the **Challenge Rating (CR)**. The CR represents the difficulty of the task, and it can be modified based on various factors like environmental conditions and the complexity of the action being attempted. In a Targeted Challenge used as a saving throw, the character is trying to resist or overcome a harmful effect, such as poison, an invocation, or a hazardous environmental condition. The CR represents the intensity or potency of the effect. If the character's roll meets or exceeds the CR, they successfully resist the effect (either partially or completely). If the roll falls below the CR, they succumb to the effect and suffer its consequences.
   * *Example (Poison Gas):* A character is exposed to a cloud of poisonous gas. The Architect calls for a **Fortitude** saving throw to resist the poison's effects. The Architect sets the **CR at 15**, considering the potency of the poison. The character rolls **2d10**, adds their Fortitude base score (`2 + 2 * Stamina`) and any other applicable bonuses, and compares the total to `CR 15`. If the total is `15` or higher, the character successfully resists the poison and avoids its harmful effects. If the total is below `15`, the character fails the check and suffers the consequences of the poison, such as taking damage or becoming *Poisoned*.
2. **Opposed Challenge (Direct Contest):**
   This type of Attribute Check involves a direct contest between two characters. Instead of comparing a character's roll to a static target number, their `2d10` roll is compared to the opposing character's `2d10` roll. The highest-scoring character wins the challenge (defender wins ties). This mechanic is used in situations where two characters are directly competing or struggling against each other, such as arm wrestling, grappling, or a battle of wills.
   * *Example (Heated Debate):* Two characters engage in a heated public debate, each trying to persuade the other to their point of view. The Architect calls for an **Opposed Challenge** using Charisma-based **Etiquette** checks. Both characters roll **2d10** and add their Etiquette score. The character with the higher total wins the debate, swaying the audience and the opponent to their side.

### 3. Skill Synergy on Attribute Checks & Saves (`+1` per `5` Over `CR 10`)

**Skill Synergy** can also come into play during attribute checks and saving throws, offering characters a chance to apply their formal skill training to enhance their innate capability in specific situations.

When a character is subjected to a harmful effect that requires a saving throw (or a complex raw attribute challenge), they can use a skill that is logically relevant to resisting or mitigating that effect. For example, a character with high **Knowledge (Medicine)** can use it to aid their **Fortitude** saving throw against a disease, or a character with high **Acrobatics** can use it to aid their **Reflex** saving throw against a trap. Another example is using the **Reason (Intellect)** check synergizing with **Knowledge (Language)** to gain a bonus when deciphering ancient inscriptions: the player rolls for **Knowledge (Language)** (`Intellect`); **for every 5 full points scored over CR 10 on the auxiliary skill check, a +1 synergy bonus is added to the Reason check**. The final Reason result is then compared to the Architect's target CR to determine if the character successfully deciphers the inscription.

The Architect decides which skills are relevant based on the situation. The skill chosen for synergy must have a clear, logical, and justifiable connection to the saving throw or attribute check being attempted:

1. **Knowledge (Language) and Reason (Intellect):** Roll **Knowledge (Language)** first; every `5` points scored over `CR 10` grants a `+1` bonus to the **Reason Check** when deciphering ancient inscriptions or alien syntax.
2. **Alertness (Perception) and Willpower (Wisdom):** When faced with an illusion or deception that attempts to manipulate the character's mind, their keen **Alertness** (`Perception`) helps them see through the sensory trickery, granting `+1` per `5` points over `CR 10` to their **Willpower Save** to resist the illusion's effects.
3. **Knowledge (Technology) and Fortitude (Stamina):** If a character is exposed to a technological hazard, such as a malfunctioning machine or a radiation leak, their **Knowledge (Technology)** helps them understand the nature of the threat and take appropriate shielding precautions, granting `+1` per `5` points over `CR 10` to their **Fortitude Save** to resist the hazard's effects.
4. **Knowledge (Medicine) and Fortitude (Stamina):** When battling a disease or poison, a character's **Knowledge (Medicine)** allows them to better understand the ailment and its physiological progression, giving them `+1` per `5` points over `CR 10` to their **Fortitude Save** to fight off the long-term effects of the illness.
5. **Athletics and Reflex (Agility):** A character's **Athletics** training and physical coordination help them react more quickly and effectively to a sudden physical danger, granting `+1` per `5` points over `CR 10` to their **Reflex Save** to avoid a particular attack or collapsing hazard.

### 4. Detailed Profiles of the Six Primary Attributes

#### I. Strength (`STR`) — Might

**Strength** measures a character's physical power, force, and stability. It is a crucial ability for tasks that involve lifting heavy objects, breaking things, and engaging in melee combat. A character with high Strength can wield heavier weapons, grapple with opponents, and resist attempts to push or knock them down.

* **Lifting and Carrying:** Strength determines how much weight a character can lift, carry, and manipulate. This is important for tasks like moving obstacles, carrying heavy equipment, or grappling with opponents.
* **Breaking Objects:** Strength is also used to break objects like doors, locks, or weapons. This is useful for opening passages, overcoming obstacles, or disarming opponents.
* **Melee Combat:** In melee combat, Strength is a key factor in determining the damage a character inflicts with melee and unarmed weapons. It also affects their ability to grapple, shove, and overpower opponents in close-quarters combat.

##### Might (`Strength`) Check / Save (`2d10 + 2 + [2 * STR]`)

This check is used to resolve actions that involve tests of raw physical power:

* **Lifting Heavy Objects:** Characters might need to lift blast gates, move boulders, or carry incapacitated allies. The heavier the object, the higher the Challenge Rating (`CR`) for the Might check.
* **Bending Bars:** This involves bending prison bars, prying open unpowered bulkhead doors, or manipulating metal objects. Material strength and desired outcome influence the CR.
* **Breaking Things:** Characters might attempt to break chains, smash through walls, or destroy structural objects. The object's durability and required force determine the CR.
* **Task Difficulty:** The Architect sets the CR for the Might check based on the difficulty of the task, taking into account factors like the weight of the object, the strength of the material, and any environmental conditions.
* **Modifiers:** Other modifiers, such as those from tools, exo-frames, equipment, or special abilities, apply to the Might check.
* **Example:** A character with a **Strength score of `+3`** attempts to bend a reinforced metal bar. The Architect sets the **CR for the Might check at 15**. The character's base score for Might checks is **+8** (`2 + [2 * 3]`). If there are no other modifiers, the character needs to roll a **7 or higher on `2d10`** to succeed.

#### II. Agility (`AGI`) — Reflex

**Agility** measures a character's balance, coordination, and nimbleness. It is a crucial attribute for tasks that involve dodging attacks, performing acrobatic feats, and engaging in ranged combat. A character with high Agility can move swiftly, react quickly to danger, and accurately target opponents with ranged weapons.

* **Dodging:** Agility determines a character's ability to dodge incoming attacks, whether in melee or ranged combat (`Passive Defense = 10 + AGI + Defense Skill + Armor/Size Mods`). This is essential for avoiding damage and staying alive in dangerous situations.
* **Acrobatics:** Agility is also used for performing acrobatic maneuvers, such as jumping, climbing, balancing, and tumbling. This is useful for navigating difficult terrain, escaping from grapples, or performing impressive feats of athleticism.
* **Ranged Combat & Initiative:** In ranged combat, Agility is a key factor in determining the accuracy of a character's attacks with ranged weapons. It also affects their Initiative, ability to reload, ready weapons quickly, and maintain balance while firing.

##### Reflex (`Agility`) Check / Save (`2d10 + 2 + [2 * AGI]`)

This check measures a character's ability to react swiftly and precisely to unexpected events or sudden changes in their environment:

* **Avoiding Attacks:** This includes area-of-effect attacks like explosions or a dragon's breath weapon, where a successful Reflex save allows the character to dive out of harm's way (halving damage, or negating it entirely with *Evasiveness*). This is crucial for survival in combat situations.
* **Catching Objects:** Reflex checks are used to determine if a character successfully catches a thrown object, grabs onto a ledge as they fall, or intercepts a moving target. This is important for both combat and non-combat situations.
* **Performing Agility Feats:** Characters with good Reflex scores are more adept at performing sudden reactive maneuvers, such as leaping over collapsing obstacles, retaining balance on narrow surfaces during tremors, or tumbling out of harm's way.
* **Task Difficulty:** The Architect sets the Challenge Rating (`CR`) for the Reflex check based on the difficulty of the task, considering factors like the speed of the attack, the size of the object being caught, or the complexity of the maneuver.
* **Modifiers:** Other modifiers, such as those from features, skills, cover, or environmental conditions, apply to the Reflex check.
* **Example:** A character with an **Agility score of `+3`** attempts to avoid a fiery blast from an explosion. The Architect sets the **CR for the Reflex save at 15**. The character's base score for Reflex checks is **+8** (`2 + [2 * 3]`). If there are no other modifiers, the character needs to roll a **7 or higher on `2d10`** to successfully dive out of harm's way.

#### III. Stamina (`STA`) — Fortitude

**Stamina** measures a character's endurance, systemic resistance, and physical toughness. A character with high Stamina can endure prolonged exertion, tolerate more damaging injuries, and resist the effects of fatigue, poison, radiation, and other debilitating conditions.

* **Enduring Physical Hardship:** Stamina determines how long a character can perform strenuous activities without succumbing to exhaustion. This includes running, swimming, climbing, and other physically demanding tasks. It also affects their ability to resist the effects of extreme temperatures, hunger, thirst, and sleep deprivation.
* **Resistances:** Stamina plays a central role in a character's ability to resist diseases, infections, radiation, and biological ailments. This is vital for surviving in harsh frontier environments or when exposed to contagious pathogens.
* **Base Toughness (Zero Stamina Pool Scaling):** A character's Stamina score serves as their **Base Toughness**, which reduces the damage taken from wounds on a **point-for-point basis** after Armor DR. **Stamina NEVER increases Vitality or Health pools** (which start at Base 30 each and scale strictly at `+5` points per `1 CP`). Stamina also sets the character's Mortality State **Stability Points (`Stamina Score + 5`)**.

##### Fortitude (`Stamina`) Check / Save (`2d10 + 2 + [2 * STA]`)

This check measures a character's ability to endure and resist physical hardships, toxins, and other debilitating effects:

* **Resisting Poison and Disease:** Characters with high Fortitude are better equipped to withstand the harmful effects of poisons, toxins, diseases, radiation, and other biological hazards. This is crucial for survival in hostile environments or when exposed to dangerous substances.
* **Enduring Extreme Environments:** Fortitude checks are also used to determine how well a character can cope with extreme temperatures, harsh weather, high gravity, and other environmental hazards. This is important for exploration, survival, and combat in challenging planetary settings.
* **Pushing Through Exhaustion and Fatigue:** Fortitude represents a character's physical and metabolic resilience, allowing them to push through exhaustion, movement fatigue, and pain. This is crucial for extended physical exertion, prolonged operational focus, or resisting physical interrogation and mortal shock.
* **Challenge Difficulty:** The Architect sets the CR for the Fortitude check based on the severity of the challenge, taking into account factors like the potency of the poison, the extremity of the temperature, or the intensity of the exertion.
* **Modifiers:** Other modifiers, such as those from environmental suits, med-stims, features, or special abilities, apply to the Fortitude check.
* **Example:** A character with a **Stamina score of `+4`** is exposed to a potent neurotoxin. The Architect sets the **CR for the Fortitude save at 18**. The character's base score for Fortitude checks is **+10** (`2 + [2 * 4]`). If there are no other modifiers, the character needs to roll an **8 or higher on `2d10`** to successfully resist the poison's effects.

#### IV. Intellect (`INT`) — Reason

**Intellect** measures a character's reason, logic, and wits. It is a crucial attribute for tasks that involve problem-solving, deduction, and understanding complex information. A character with high Intellect can analyze situations quickly, come up with creative solutions, and excel in fields that require mental acuity, technical engineering, or Arcane/Psychic metaphysics.

* **Problem-Solving:** Intellect determines a character's ability to analyze problems, identify patterns, and devise effective solutions. This is important for overcoming obstacles, solving puzzles, and strategizing in challenging situations.
* **Deduction:** Intellect is also used for making deductions and drawing logical conclusions based on available information. This is useful for forensic investigations, uncovering hidden truths, and predicting the actions of others.
* **Understanding Complex Information:** Intellect allows a character to comprehend and process complex information, such as technical manuals, scientific theories, computer code, or ancient texts, and contributes directly to **Perception Base (`INT + WIS`)**. This is essential for fields like science, engineering, and medicine.

##### Reason (`Intellect`) Check / Save (`2d10 + 2 + [2 * INT]`)

This check assesses a character's capacity for logical thinking, problem-solving, and comprehension of complex information. Reason checks are called for in situations where a character must analyze a problem, identify patterns, deduce solutions, or understand intricate concepts. High Intellect scores significantly enhance a character's performance in these checks:

* **Puzzles and Riddles:** Characters might encounter puzzles, algorithmic locks, or riddles that require logical deduction and problem-solving skills to decipher. The complexity of the puzzle and the time pressure involved affect the Challenge Rating (`CR`) of the Reason check.
* **Deciphering Codes and Languages:** Characters with strong Reason abilities are better at cracking codes, deciphering cryptic transmissions, and analyzing unfamiliar linguistic structures. The complexity of the code or language and the available clues determine the CR.
* **Understanding Complex Information:** Characters might need to comprehend dense technical manuals, precursor blueprints, scientific theories, or ancient texts. The density of the information and the character's existing knowledge base affect the CR.
* **Task Difficulty:** The Architect determines the CR for the Reason check based on the complexity of the task, considering factors like the intricacy of the puzzle, the obscurity of the code, or the density of the information.
* **Modifiers:** Other modifiers, such as those from skills, features, processor tools, or databases, apply to the Reason check. Reason checks offer a versatile and engaging way to incorporate intellectual challenges into the game, allowing characters to showcase their problem-solving abilities and mental acuity.
* **Example:** A character with an **Intellect score of `+4`** attempts to solve a riddle that guards a hidden precursor vault. The Architect sets the **CR for the Reason check at 20**. The character's base score for Reason checks is **+10** (`2 + [2 * 4]`). If there are no other modifiers, the character needs to roll a **10 or higher on `2d10`** to successfully solve the riddle.

#### V. Wisdom (`WIS`) — Willpower

**Wisdom** measures a character's insight, intuition, and determination. It is a crucial attribute for tasks that involve sensing deception, resisting fear, and understanding the motivations of others. A character with high Wisdom can perceive hidden truths, remain calm in the face of danger, and make sound judgments even in difficult situations, while anchoring Divine and Primal metaphysical traditions.

* **Sensing Deception:** Wisdom determines a character's ability to detect lies, inconsistencies, and hidden agendas (`Insight`). This is important for negotiations, interrogations, and social interactions where trust is a factor.
* **Resisting Fear:** Wisdom also plays a role in a character's ability to resist fear, panic, and emotional manipulation. This is crucial for staying focused and making rational decisions in dangerous or stressful situations.
* **Understanding Motivations:** Wisdom allows a character to understand the underlying motivations and desires of others and contributes to **Perception Base (`INT + WIS`)**. This is useful for predicting their actions, finding common ground, and navigating complex social dynamics.

##### Willpower (`Wisdom`) Check / Save (`2d10 + 2 + [2 * WIS]`)

This check represents a character's mental fortitude, resilience, and strength of mind. Willpower saves are called upon when a character faces situations that test their determination, resolve, and ability to resist internal or external pressures. A high Wisdom score significantly enhances a character's performance in these checks:

* **Resisting Fear:** When confronted with terrifying creatures, eldritch phenomena, dangerous situations, or overwhelming odds, characters must rely on their Willpower to overcome fear, panic, or despair. This is crucial for maintaining composure, making rational decisions, and taking effective actions in the face of danger.
* **Overcoming Mental Manipulation:** Characters might encounter enemies or entities that attempt to control their minds, manipulate their emotions, or influence their actions through psychic powers, magic, or other means. Willpower saves determine a character's ability to resist such manipulation, maintain their autonomy, and break free from mental domination.
* **Maintaining Focus Under Pressure:** In high-stress situations, such as tense negotiations, complex problem-solving, or channeling invocations under fire, characters must rely on their Willpower to stay focused, avoid distractions, and maintain their concentration. #### VI. Charisma (`CHA`) — Etiquette

**Charisma** measures a character's confidence, assertiveness, and personal magnetism. It is a crucial attribute for tasks that involve persuasion, leadership, and social interaction. A character with high Charisma can influence and inspire others, negotiate effectively, and excel in roles that require social finesse, while setting their Negative Karma limit (`CHA + 1`) and governing Hereditary/Bardic metaphysics.

* **Social Interaction:** Charisma allows a character to navigate social situations with ease, making a positive impression and building rapport with others. This is useful for gathering information, making allies, and avoiding conflicts.
