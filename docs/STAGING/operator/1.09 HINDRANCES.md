### **1. Hindrances Overview (Unabridged Codex Introduction)**

**Hindrances in Tangent are character flaws, quirks, or hindrances that provide additional Character Points (CP) during character creation. These can add depth, realism, and role-playing opportunities to a character, but they also come with drawbacks or limitations.**

* **Types of Hindrances:** Hindrances can be physical, mental, social, or a combination of these. Examples include phobias, addictions, chronic illnesses, physical disabilities, or even personality traits like impulsiveness or arrogance.
* **CP Value:** Each Hindrance has a Character Point (`CP`) value assigned to it, reflecting the severity of the drawback it imposes. More significant flaws grant more CP, allowing players to invest in other areas of their character.
* **Balancing Act & The +10 CP Personal Hindrance Cap:** While Hindrances offer a way to gain extra CP, players should be mindful not to overload their characters with too many flaws. A character with numerous severe hindrances might become difficult or unfun to play. Under canonical character creation rules (`1.01 CHARACTER CREATION.md`), the maximum bonus gained from **personal Hindrances is strictly capped at `+10 CP`** (while the legacy Codex narrative notes a `15 CP` tragic campaign ceiling only when explicitly authorized by the Architect). **Innate species-level disadvantages, biological flaws, and Xeno Stigmas (`1.03 SPECIES.md`) are explicitly EXEMPT from this `+10 CP` personal Hindrance cap**, as they are factored directly into the net Species Package CP cost.
* **Role-Playing Opportunities:** Hindrances can create interesting role-playing opportunities and challenges. Players can explore how their character's flaws affect their interactions with others, their decision-making, and their overall approach to the game world.
* **Architect (GM) Involvement:** The Architect plays a crucial role in approving Hindrances and ensuring they are appropriate for the setting and the character's concept. They may also offer suggestions or work with the player to develop unique Hindrances that fit the character's background and personality.
* **Example (Acrophobia):** A character with a fear of heights (*Acrophobia — Moderate Emotional Issues*) might gain **6 CP**, reflecting the significant impact this phobia can have on their actions and choices. This could lead to interesting role-playing moments, such as the character hesitating to climb a tall tower or panicking (`Willpower Save CR 15`) when crossing a narrow bridge.

Hindrances are an optional but valuable tool for character creation in Tangent. They enable players to create more complex, nuanced, and memorable characters, and they can enrich the role-playing experience by introducing challenges and opportunities for growth.

### **2. Hindrances Compendium & System Mechanics**

#### **System Overview**

In the Tangent Role-Playing System, **Hindrances** are negative traits, flaws, or hindrances that a character possesses. Unlike standard Features which cost Character Points (`3 CP` Standard / `2 CP` Recommended), Hindrances **grant** additional CP during character creation. This allows players to "buy" more positive traits, skills, or attribute scores by accepting specific limitations.

#### **Design Philosophy**

* **Depth & Realism:** Hindrances are designed to flesh out a character's personality and history. A character with a phobia, a debt, or a missing limb is often more narrative-rich than a "perfect" character.
* **Role-Playing Incentives:** These flaws provide hooks for the Architect (Game Master) to create complications, driving the story forward.
* **The Balancing Act:** While tempting for the extra points, players are cautioned against taking too many. A character crippled by flaws may become unplayable or a burden to the party.

#### **Mechanics & Limits**

* **Character Points (CP):** Each Hindrance has a specific CP value (e.g., `2`, `3`, `6`, `9`, `12`, `18`, `30 CP`) based on its severity. Up to **`+10 CP`** from personal Hindrances is added to the character's starting customization pool.
* **Canonical Personal Cap (`+10 CP`) & Species Exemption:** The canonical limit for personal Hindrances is **`+10 CP`**. Players may take higher-point Hindrances (such as `12 CP` Severe Issues or `18 CP` Quadriplegic Disability) or multiple flaws for narrative depth, but the total bonus CP gained cannot exceed `+10 CP` (unless the Architect authorizes a high-flaw/tragic campaign variant up to `15 CP`). **Species-level Hindrances and Stigmas are exempt from this cap.**
* **Architect Discretion:** The Architect has the final say on whether a Hindrance is valid for the campaign setting. A "Fear of Water" Hindrance grants no free points in a desert campaign and may be disallowed or reduced in value.

#### **Terminology Distinction (`Hindrance` vs. `Disadvantage`)**

* **Important:** Do not confuse **Hindrances (Character Flaws)** with the **Rolling with Disadvantage** dice mechanic.
* **Hindrances (Capital H):** Permanent or semi-permanent character traits chosen at creation (e.g., *Blindness*, *Debt*, *Addiction*).
* **Rolling with Disadvantage (Situational Dice Mechanic):** The core resolution mechanic where a player rolls an extra `d10` per stack of Disadvantage on their `2d10` check and keeps the **lowest 2 dice** (`2d10 + 1d10` keep lowest 2).
* *Note:* Several Character Hindrances (such as *Impaired Sense*, *Not-Disguisable Distinctive Features*, or *Major/Severe Social Issues*) cause a player to roll with **Disadvantage** on specific checks.

### **3. The Seven Thematic Categories of Hindrances**

Hindrances are grouped into seven primary categories to help define the source of the trouble:

1. **I. Background Hindrances:** *Relating to the character's past history, upbringing, or previous entanglements.*
   * **Examples:** Dark Secret, Wanted by the Law (Mundane), Non-Monetary Debt, Sworn Enemy (Adversary), Traumatic Past (Triggers), Unwanted Legacy.
2. **II. Mental Hindrances:** *Relating to cognitive function, memory, or intellectual barriers.*
   * **Examples:** Poor Memory, Learning Disability, Arrogance, Paranoia, Impaired Reasoning, Low Self-Esteem, Mental Issues, Nightmares, Technologically Impaired.
3. **III. Metaphysical Hindrances:** *Relating to magic, psionics, the soul, or supernatural forces.*
   * **Examples:** Extreme Morality, Unlucky / Karmic Debt, Curses, Divine Disfavor, Haunted, Magical Vulnerability (e.g., Cold Iron), Geas/Pact restrictions, "Weirdness Magnet."
4. **IV. Physical Hindrances:** *Relating to the body, health, and sensory capabilities.*
   * **Examples:** Bodily Age, Missing Limbs (Disability), Distinctive Features, Blindness/Deafness (Lost Sense), Impaired Sense, Mute, Chronic Illness, Frailty, Allergies, Physical Deformities.
5. **V. Psychological Hindrances:** *Relating to behavior patterns, compulsions, and personality restrictions.*
   * **Examples:** Phobias, Addictions, Obsessions (Covetous), Emotional Issues, Hatred, Strict Codes of Honor (Honorable), Vows of Pacifism/Silence, Impulsiveness, Greed, Laziness.
6. **VI. Resource Hindrances:** *Relating to assets, wealth, and equipment.*
   * **Examples:** Poverty (Poor), Massive Financial Debt, Lack of Shelter, Dependency on Rare Substances (Maintenance).
7. **VII. Social Hindrances:** *Relating to standing in society, reputation, and relationships.*
   * **Examples:** Bad Reputation, Clown, Humorless, Minority / Xeno Stigma, Socially Awkward (Social Issues), Dependent (NPC), Obligation / Debt of Honor.
