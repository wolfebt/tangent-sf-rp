## **INTRODUCTION**

The construction of a believable science fiction or fantasy setting requires a rigorous integration of narrative philosophy, visual aesthetics, and mechanical function. In Tangent, architecture serves not merely as a backdrop but as a tangible representation of a faction’s technological capability (Tech Level), cultural values, and tactical priorities.  
A building is a static unit of gameplay potential—defined by its structural integrity, the facilities it houses, and the cover it provides during combat scenarios. Architecture in speculative fiction acts as a silent narrator. The transition from the "Gilded Cage" aesthetic of the Imperial Brutalist palace to the "Visual Noise" of an Entropist slum communicates the distribution of power and resource scarcity more effectively than exposition.

### 

### **The Tangent Construction System**

The Tangent Construction System allows players and GMs to design, build, and manage structures ranging from simple sheds to sprawling arcologies. Structures are defined by three primary factors: **Footprint** (Base Size), **Verticality** (Height), and **Materials** (Tech Level).

### 

### **Economic Integration**

In alignment with the Tangent Economic Matrix, the complexity of a structure (**Crafting DC**) directly dictates its monetary value. The relationship is exponential, defined by the Tangent Standard Curve formula:  
> **Value (Credits) \= 10 \* 4^(DC / 5\)**  
*Note: This Value represents the raw construction and material cost. The "Real Estate" market price (Land Value) is a separate modifier based on Planetary Population and Law Level (World Codes).*

## **I. THE UNIVERSAL SCALE: MODULES VS. MOUNTS**

To ensure absolute mathematical consistency across personal inventory, vehicular engineering, and architectural design, the matrix relies upon the **Universal Displacement Unit (UDU)** hierarchy.  
When designing architecture, ships, or planetary macro-structures, builders must manage two distinct tiers of capacity: **Modules** and **Mounts**.

### 

### **Tier 3: The Module (Rooms & Facilities)**

The **Module** is the baseline unit of macro-scale structural volume (representing roughly 400 square feet of functional space, or a 20x20 ft area, up to a Maximum 10 Tons displacement). In architectural terms, a Module represents a physical room or a massive internal facility—such as a generator room, a barracks, a medical bay, or a vehicle hangar. A building's total Size and Height determine how many Modules it can hold.

### 

### **Tier 2: The Mount (Hardpoints & Sub-Scale Gear)**

The **Mount** is a sub-scale hardpoint (Maximum 100 kg displacement). In architectural and capital-ship design, Mounts are used for functional components that do not require an entire room. This includes exterior structural armor plating, energy shield emitters, defense turrets, sensor dishes, and localized utility nodes.

### 

### **The 10:1 Integration Rule**

The UDU hierarchy relies on a strict 10:1 conversion ratio. **1 Module equals 10 Usable Mounts.** When constructing a base, an architect can dedicate a full Module to a room, or they can partition that Module into 10 external or internal Mounts to install heavy defenses, armor, and specialized gear.

| Scale Category | Architectural Application | Integration Bottleneck |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Tier 1: Socket** | Personal gear, handheld items. | — |
| **Tier 2: Mount** | Hardpoints: Armor, Weapons, Shields, Sensors. | 1 Mount \= 10 Sockets |
| **Tier 3: Module** | Facilities: Rooms, Hangars, Cores, Barracks. | 1 Module \= 10 Mounts |

### 

### **Dimensional Footprint and Base Complexity**

Every discrete piece of equipment is assigned a Size Category, which dictates its base Structure Points (SP) and its baseline Crafting Difficulty Class (DC). This establishes the unflavored mechanical reality of the Agnostic Chassis.

| Size | Common Examples | Mass | Capacity | Structure | Crafting DC |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Fine** | Data-chip, Piercing | \< 0.1 kg | (2 Nodes) | 2 SP | DC 5 (Simple) |
| **Diminutive** | Datapad, Sensor Fob | \< 1 kg | 1 Socket | 5 SP | DC 5 (Simple) |
| **Tiny** | Pistol, Medkit | \< 5 kg | 2 Sockets | 10 SP | DC 10 (Standard) |
| **Small** | Rifle, Enviro-Suit | \< 10 kg | 4 Sockets | 20 SP | DC 15 (Advanced) |
| **Medium** | Heavy Tool, Generator | \< 25 kg | 8 Sockets | 40 SP | DC 20 (Expert) |
| **Mecha** | Emplacement, Turret | \< 100 kg | Mounts | 100 SP (Medium) | DC 30 (Grandmaster) |
| **Structure** | Building, Laboratory | \> 10 Tons | Modules | 500+ SP | Variable |

> *Items classified as "Fine" possess a mass so negligible they do not consume standard Socket capacity for inventory management. They may serve as minor aesthetic additions or highly miniaturized components, though they remain subject to standard durability constraints.*

### 

### **Garaging Mecha in Architecture**

Lower-sized mecha garaging is based on the displaced mass of the Mecha vs the Module (at 10 Tons of capacity). For large ships and bases, use the conversion of 10 Mounts to 1 Module for ease of calculation.

* **Medium** (Human Size x1 Scale, \<8ft): Requires \~0.025 Modules (40 units fit in 1 Module).  
* **Large** (x2 Scale, \<16ft): Requires 0.50 Modules (2 units fit in 1 Module).  
* **Huge** (x5 Scale, \<32ft): Requires 5 Full Modules (Hangar Bay) per unit.  
* **Gargantuan** (x10 Scale, \<64ft): Requires 10 Full Modules (Heavy Bay) per unit.  
* **Colossal** (x20 Scale, \<128ft): Requires 20 Full Modules (Launch Gantry) per unit.

### 

### **2\. Verticality (Height Scaling)**

To create tall structures (towers, skyscrapers, apartments), apply a Height Class modifier to the Base Footprint. This determines the Total Modules, Total Structure Points, and increases the DC (and thus Value).

* **Total Modules** \= Base Modules \* Stories  
* **Total SP** \= Base SP \* Stories  
* **Total Craft DC** \= Base Craft DC \+ Craft Modifier

| Height Class | Stories | Examples | Craft Mod |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Single** | 1 | Ranch House, Shed, Warehouse | \+0 |
| **Duplex** | 2 | 2-Story House, Townhouse | \+2 |
| **Multi-Story** | 3-5 | Apartment Block, Office | \+4 |
| **Mid-Rise** | 6-12 | Corporate HQ, Hotel | \+8 |
| **High-Rise** | 13-40 | Urban Tower | \+12 |
| **Skyscraper** | 40+ | Mega-Tower, Spire | \+16 |

## **III. THE FRAMEWORK OF ERAS AND TECHNOLOGICAL SUB-STRATA**

The efficiency, durability, and operational methodology of a structure's chassis are explicitly gated by its **Tech Level (TL)**.

* **TL 1-2 (Analog):** Stamped Metal, Concrete, Plastic. **Passive Trait:** None.  
* **TL 3 (Digital):** Plasteel, Aluminum, Composite. **Passive Trait:** Modular (Universal ports; shared utility grids).  
* **TL 4 (Enhanced):** Smart-Fabric, Nanocarbon. **Passive Trait:** Self-Repairing (Regens 1 SP/hour). Mastery of Force/Directed Energy shielding.  
* **TL 5 (Advanced):** Polymatter, Hard-Light. **Passive Trait:** Morphic / Weightless (Instant and functional mass projection).

### 

### **Era Summaries & DC Modifiers**

* **TL 0: The Stone Age:** Wood, stone, hide. **Trait:** Degrading (Hardness/DR 5, x0.50 SP Multiplier). **Base DC Mod:** \-10 DC.  
* **TL 1: The Metal Age:** Brick, iron-beam framing. Steam/Coal power. (Hardness/DR 10, x0.75 SP Multiplier). **Base DC Mod:** \-5 DC.  
* **TL 2: The Data Age:** Steel-reinforced concrete, glass. Hydrocarbon/fission power. (Hardness/DR 15, x1.0 SP Multiplier). **Base DC Mod:** \-2 DC.  
* **TL 3: The Space Age:** Plasteel, Duranium. Fusion power. Hermetic seals standard. (Hardness/DR 20, x2.0 SP Multiplier). **Base DC Mod:** \+0 DC.  
* **TL 4: The Stellar Age:** Smart-fabrics, nanocarbon, crystal-matrix walls. Repulsorlift foundations. (Hardness/DR 30, x3.0 SP Multiplier). **Base DC Mod:** \+5 DC.  
* **TL 5: The Galactic Age:** Polymatter, Hard-light emitters. Dimensional compression. (Hardness/DR 50+, x5.0 SP Multiplier). **Base DC Mod:** \+10 DC.

## **IV. PLANETARY & ENVIRONMENTAL MODIFIERS**

Construction is not performed in a vacuum. The specific conditions of a world impose modifiers on the Design and Construction phases.

* **Low Gravity (\<0.8G):** Structures can be taller and lighter. \-2 DC to Design checks. Verticality Craft Modifiers are halved.  
* **High Gravity (\>1.5G):** Structures require reinforced foundations. \+5 DC to Design/Build checks. Verticality Craft Modifiers are doubled. Failure typically results in collapse during the build phase.  
* **Vacuum / Toxic / Corrosive:** Requires "Life Support" Module. Total Construction Cost increases by 20% for hermetic sealing and airlocks *(Note: Standard and free on TL3+ structures).*  
* **Liquid (Aquatic):** Requires "Pressure Hull" reinforcement. \+5 DC. Total Cost increases by 50%.

## **V. STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION AND MASTERCRAFTING**

Architecture in Tangent is inherently modular, capable of evolving alongside its inhabitants through the utilization of UDU Capacity.

### 

### **The Highest Complexity Rule (DC Stacking)**

To prevent abstract exponential cost bloat, mundane rooms (barracks, standard cargo, basic generators) **do not** individually increase the building's Crafting DC; their complexity is absorbed by the Base Footprint DC.  
However, if a player installs a highly advanced specialized module (such as a Holodeck DC 35 or an Antimatter Core DC 30\) into a building with a lower overall DC, **the building's total Base DC is raised to match the highest complex component installed**.  
Certain structural modifications apply flat penalties to the final DC:

* **Mastercrafting (Bonus Capacity):** For every **5 points** an Acquisition or Crafting check exceeds the building's base DC, the structure gains **1 Bonus UDU Slot** (e.g., 10 Bonus Mounts or 1 Bonus Module of capacity, up to a maximum of 1 Bonus per Tech Level of the item/structure).  
* **UDU Compression (Spatial Engineering):**  
  * **Efficient Design:** \-25% UDU Capacity cost (Round up). Adds \+5 DC.  
  * **Miniaturized:** \-50% UDU Capacity cost (Round up). Requires Tech Level \+1 over the structure's base TL. Adds \+10 DC.  
  * **Integrated:** 0 UDU Capacity (Fused seamlessly into the chassis/foundation). Cannot be removed or swapped later. Adds \+5 DC.

## **VI. META-TECH INTEGRATION: SYNTHESIZING SCIENCE AND SORCERY**

In the Tangent Universe, the divide between science and sorcery is treated purely as a matter of origin, not effect. **Meta-Tech** represents the engineering necessary to accommodate reality-warping patterns within physical Sockets, Mounts, and Modules.

| Physical Unit | Operational Scale | Capacity Limit for Invocations |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1 Socket** | Personal Scale | Holds up to Rank 10 (Trained Effect) |
| **2 Sockets** | Personal Scale | Holds up to Rank 20 (Master Effect) |
| **1 Mount** | Vehicle / Hardpoint | Holds any Rank up to Pinnacle (Rank 30\) |
| **1 Module** | Strategic / Facility | Holds any Rank up to Pinnacle (Rank 30\) |

### 

### **The Principle of Scale**

This principle dictates that an Invocation installed in a larger chassis is fundamentally more potent. A Rank 10 *Teleport* installed in a personal suit (1 Socket) moves an individual 5 miles; the same Rank 10 *Teleport* installed in a Mecha (1 Mount) moves a 10-ton vehicle 25 miles. When installed in an Architectural Module (Strategic Scale), that same Rank 10 *Teleport* utilizes the building's massive power grid to move an entire fortress or colony structure.  
Meta-Tech serves three distinct structural engineering goals:

* **Enhancement (Passive):** "Always On" improvements. Each UDU allocated increases the structure's Crafting DC by \+5.  
* **Imbuement (Active):** Hard-coding a spell into a building's mainframe to be cast via command console. Crafting DC \= 15 \+ Invocation Rank \+ TL Modifier (TL3: \+0, TL4: \+2, TL5: \+5).  
* **Interface (Symbiotic):** Facilities that amplify an Awakened user's natural skills. The Invocation's output is multiplied by the Chassis Scale (e.g., casting through a Colossal Arcology x20 Scale deals twenty times the damage).

## **VII. SPECIALIZED STRUCTURAL ECOSYSTEMS & BIOTECHNOLOGY**

### **Advanced Grown Architecture (Biotech Protocols)**

Factions utilizing grown biotechnology (like the Auluran and Thorn) subvert traditional power grid mechanics. Grown tech is subject to three core protocols:

1. **Symbiosis (Bonding):** Living facilities must bio-electrically "bond" with a host over 24-hours.  
2. **Sustenance (Feeding):** Grown architecture requires nutrient consumption. Heavy defensive bio-emplacements must be fully irrigated with Nutrient Solution once per week, lest they degrade.  
3. **Bio-Repair:** Damage to living architecture is healed via Medicine or Xenobiology checks. A healthy bonded Overseer facilitates the automatic regeneration of 1 SP per hour.

**Specific Factional Architectural Innovations:**

* **Empathic Walls (Auluran):** Reactive structural armor that adapts when struck by energy, granting DR \+5 to the building's exterior against that specific energy type for the remainder of the Scene/Siege.  
* **Chorist Hub (Auluran):** A grown neural-graft transceiver module. Allows the Overseer to maintain Telepathy within a 10-mile radius.  
* **Sunglass Canopies (Alterian):** Crystalline structural roofs grown from silica and light. Acts as a massive solar capacitor, recharging internal energy reserves automatically.  
* **Hive-Resin (Kitin):** A secreted construction material lighter than plasteel but self-sealing. Grants \+2 to Stealth (sound dampening) for those operating inside.

### 

### **Programmable Matter and Structural Utility**

Programmable Matter, known as "Picotech" at the structural scale (TL 4, DC 25), consists of reservoirs of smart sand or fluid metal built into the foundation. It can be commanded to reshape hallways, deploy internal cover, or generate Simple tools directly from the walls.

## **VIII. THE ARCHITECTURAL FORGE PROTOCOL (INSTRUCTIONAL GUIDE)**

* **Who:** Game Masters constructing world assets; Players engaging in civil engineering or base-building.  
* **What:** A rigorous, sequential design matrix that explicitly links physical footprint, technological complexity, and structural utility to Wealth Score (WS) and Build Time.  
* **Where/When:** Utilized during pre-game preparation or downtime crafting sessions.  
* **Why:** To ensure that all static assets remain mathematically viable and economically balanced.  
* **How:** Execute the following step-by-step construction protocol.

### 

### **Step 1: Footprint & Scale (Size)**

Structures define their size by their Footprint and their capacity by Modules. The Base DC (Section II) represents the Crafting DC for a single-story chassis at Tech Level 3\. Determine the Verticality (Height Scaling) to lock in the Base Size DC and total Module Capacity.

### 

### **Step 2: Frame Type & Configuration**

The physical form of the structure dictates its utility and its Complexity Modifier, which is added to the Base DC to determine the final cost.

| Frame | Examples | Modifiers | Complexity |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Industrial** | Factories, Warehouses | Function over form; boxy, bare concrete/metal. | \-2 DC |
| **Standard** | Civilian Housing, Shops | Basic habitation with standard utilities. | \+0 DC |
| **Elevated** | Built on Stilts, Platforms | \+25% Modules (expand outward), \-25% SP (foundation stress). | \+2 DC |
| **Tower** | Spires, Skyscrapers | Verticality focus, wind-shear resistant. | \+2 DC |
| **Subterranean** | Bunkers, Vaults | Dug into earth/rock. \+15% SP, \-15% Modules (hard to expand). | \+5 DC |
| **Biomimetic** | Grown Arcologies | Mimics local flora/geography; highly customized. | \+5 DC |
| **Dynamic** | Mekan Geometry | Modularly shifting rooms and hallways. | \+5 DC |
| **Palatial** | Castles, Megacorps | Highly versatile, prioritizing defense and extreme luxury. | \+8 DC |

### 

### **Step 3: Core Infrastructure (Free Systems)**

The following systems are integral to the building chassis and DO NOT count toward the capacity limit:

* **Primary Foundation:** Concrete slab, deep pilings, or grav-anchors.  
* **Basic Locomotion/Access:** Standard hallways, stairs, and doors.  
* **Basic Utility Grid:** Ambient lighting, standard climate control, and basic plumbing.

### 

### **Step 4: Facilities & Hardpoints (Module & Mount Allocation)**

Allocate your building's total capacity into Rooms (Modules) and Sub-Scale Hardpoints (Mounts).

* **Allocate Modules:** Select major facilities from Section IX. Each room consumes full Modules.  
* **Partition Mounts:** Take any unspent Modules and convert them into Mounts (1 Module \= 10 Mounts). Use these Mounts to install Structural Armor, Energy Shields, Defense Turrets, and Sensors.

### 

### **Step 5: Finalization & The Productivity Engine (Build Time)**

Establish the Final DC of the chassis by combining the Footprint DC, Frame Modifier, Environmental Modifiers, and checking against the "Highest Complexity Rule" (if an installed module has a higher DC than the base, the building's DC raises to match it). Calculate final Structure Points, Value, and Build Time.

* **Final SP:** Base SP \* Height Stories \* Tech Level SP Multiplier  
* **Value:** Value (Credits) \= 10 \* 4^(DC/5)  
* **Build Time:** The Target Productivity Points (PP) required to finish the structure equals its Credit Value.  
* **Daily Progress:** (PP) \= (Craft Check Result \- 10\) \* Tool Tier Multiplier \* Crew Size

**TOOL TIERS & MULTIPLIERS**

| Tech Tier | Tools Required | Multiplier | Narrative Equivalent |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **0** | Improvised | x1 | Stone tools, bare hands. |
| **1** | Basic | x10 | Handheld power tools, cement mixer. |
| **2** | Advanced | x50 | Cranes, excavators, heavy machines. |
| **3** | Industrial | x200 | Auto-factories, constructor drones. |
| **4** | Nanoforge | x1,000 | Molecular assembly swarms. |
| **Bio** | Cultivation | x1,000 | Hyper-Growth Vats. Requires Medicine/Nature and Engineering checks. |
| **5** | Genesis | x5,000 | Thought-responsive Polymatter loom, Metaphysical fabrication. |

## **XI. VARIABLE FORM TECHNOLOGY (VFT)**

**Variable Form Technology** allows structural units to reconfigure their physical chassis to adapt to combat conditions, bridging the gap between high-mobility vehicle modes, static defensive structures, and versatile biomimetic forms.

### 

### **1\. VFT Tech Level Progression**

The efficiency, speed, and method of these transformations are strictly gated by Tech Level (TL).

* **TL3: Mechanical Articulation (The "Hard" Shift):** Hydraulic actuators, massive gears, and sliding rails. Slow transformation (Full Round Action), and the structure is highly vulnerable during the shift.  
* **TL4: Programmable Matter (The "Fluid" Shift):** "Smart-Matter" composites and picotech integration. Rapid transformation (Movement Action), and can occur mid-maneuver.  
* **TL5: Polymatter & Holophotonics (The "State" Shift):** Sub-atomic rearrangement or hard-light projection. Near Instantaneous (Swift Action).

### 

### **2\. Alternate Form Classifications**

* **Form Type A: Structural Integration (Bastion Mode):** Transformation from a mobile vehicle/mech into a static defensive emplacement. The structure gains \+100% Armor DR, but suffers \-100% Speed (Immobile).  
* **Form Type B: Biomimetic Configuration (Mecha/Beast Mode):** Transformation from a static structure or standard vehicle into a humanoid or animalistic form optimized for terrain traversal. Gains Melee capabilities, Dodge/Evasion bonuses, but suffers \-50% Armor DR.  
* **Form Type C: Vehicular Configuration (Interceptor Mode):** Transformation into a streamlined vehicle for rapid transit. Optimized entirely for Speed and Momentum over utility or defense.

### 

### **3\. VFT Design & Cost Protocols**

When an architect or player engineer aims to design a Variable Form Structure, the following protocols must be strictly adhered to:  
**Multi-Form Efficiency (Capacity Loss)** Transforming chassis require internal tracks, shifting gears, and redundant skeletal integrity.

* **Rule:** Impose a **10% reduction of total available Mounts/Capacity** for each EXTRA FORM added beyond the primary chassis mode.

**Differential Costing (Valuation Protocol)** You do not simply double the cost of the structure, but you must account for the distinct, functioning components of each form.

* **Step 1:** Calculate the full Credit Value for each form separately (including all modules, weapons, and speeds active in that specific form).  
* **Step 2:** Identify the most expensive design; this serves as the foundational base price of the structure.  
* **Step 3:** Apply a **20% reduction** to the calculated cost of any additional forms.  
* **Step 4:** Summarize the values (Base Price \+ Discounted Additional Forms) to discover the final, singular Wealth Score (WS) total required to acquire or build the VFT structure.

## **XII. STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY & COMBAT**

Buildings have Structure Points (SP) representing their total integrity, but tactical gameplay often focuses on localized damage.

### 

### **Combat Modifier Logic**

* **Attacking Smaller:** Larger creatures/structures deal massive damage but have lower defense against small, nimble targets.  
* **Attacking Larger:** Smaller targets have Advantage on defense/dodge but deal insignificant damage to massive structures unless using Heavy Weapons.

### 

### **Breaching vs. Destroying**

* **Section Integrity:** A 10x10ft section of wall has roughly 10% of the Building's Base SP.  
* **Breach:** Creating a hole for a Medium creature requires dealing damage \> Section Integrity to a specific 5ft section (after Hardness/DR is applied).  
* **Collapse:** Reducing the Total SP of the entire structure to 0 results in catastrophic failure and collapse.

### 

### **Maintenance & Repair Cycle**

Structures are not eternal. Weather, entropy, and use degrade them.

* **Annual Upkeep:** 2% of Total Value per year. Failure to pay results in "Dilapidated" status (-5 DR, \-10% SP).  
* **Repair Action:** Field Patch requires Knowledge (Engineering) or Vocation (Builder) check DC 15\. Restores 10% SP per hour. Cost: 10% of Building Value in materials. Reconstruction is required if SP \< 0 (Collapse).

## **XV. ECONOMIC INTEGRATION: THE TANGENT STANDARD ECONOMICS**

The most significant advancement in the Tangent Framework is the complete eradication of economic dissonance—the historically persistent fracture between the narrative abstraction of wealth and the mechanical reality of crafting and buying items. The Tangent Standard Economics posits a singular, mathematically rigorous solution: **Complexity Determines Value.** The market price of a structure or item is simply the societal aggregate of the skill (Crafting DC) and time required to produce it.

### 

### **15.1 The Tangent Standard Curve (TSC)**

Because the gap between a simple survival tent and a sprawling moon base is logarithmic, the cost scaling must be exponential. The market price of every physical asset within the Tangent galaxy is generated using the Tangent Standard Curve (TSC) formula.  
This formula dictates that an item's value in Credits equals a baseline value of 10 Credits (representing the simplest manufactured good or scrap metal at DC 0\) multiplied by a growth factor of 4, raised to the power of the item's Crafting DC divided by 5\. The growth factor of 4 ensures that value inherently quadruples across every tier interval of 5 DC.  
> **Value (Credits) \= 10 \* 4^(DC / 5\)**  
By strictly adhering to this calculation, all pricing debates are eliminated. This mathematical rigidity allows Game Masters to value any new, homebrew, or precursor architectural artifact instantly simply by assigning it a Crafting DC based on its operational complexity.

### 

### **15.3 The Liquidity Gap and Friction Mechanics**

A common failure point in abstract wealth systems is the "infinite money loop," where players attempt to use their passive Wealth Score to acquire high-value items for free and immediately sell them for infinite cash. Tangent mitigates this via strict friction mechanics, ensuring "Loot" (liquid Credits) remains a vital gameplay reward at all tiers.

**The Liquidity Constraint (The Gap Rule):**  
When a character seeks to build or buy an asset with a DC exceeding their Wealth Score, their passive income cannot cover the transaction. They must bridge the gap with liquid Credits. The required liquid cost is calculated by subtracting the Credit value of the character's Wealth Score from the Credit value of the desired item's DC.  
> **Cost (Liquid) \= Value (Item DC) \- Value (Wealth Score)**  
*Example:* A Wealth Score 15 character (Auto-buy limit \~640 Cr) wants to construct a Secure Frontier Outpost (DC 18, Value \~1,470 Cr). They use their WS 15 leverage to cover the base material cost, leaving 830 Credits that must be paid in liquid cash from their adventuring savings or corporate backing.

**Liquidity Drag (The Fence Rate):**  
To further prevent market abuse, the economy imposes Liquidity Drag. In real-world economics and RPG simulations, the "Buy" price and "Sell" price are never identical due to fencing effort, market saturation, and legality. The Sell Price of an asset is heavily reduced:

* **Legal goods and registered Real Estate:** Sell at 50% of Total Value.  
* **Black market, stolen goods, or unregistered Habs:** Sell at 20-25% of Total Value.  
* **Scrap and Salvage:** Sells at 10% of Total Value.

Because the raw materials required to craft or construct an asset also cost 50% of the item's Total Value, a character who buys materials and builds a structure for immediate resale operates at a **0% profit margin**. Profit is only possible through adventuring, scavenging materials for free, or utilizing specialized merchant skills to negotiate better rates.

### 

### **15.4 The Productivity Engine and Macro-Economics**

Because the Tangent Standard Curve dictates that value scales exponentially, a linear crafting system (e.g., generating 50 credits of value per day) would require literal centuries to build high-end assets, ending campaigns before structures are finished. Tangent solves the "Time vs. Cost" paradox through the Productivity Engine, converting effort into Productivity Points (PP).

**The Productivity Formula:**  
To construct a building, the creator must accumulate a Target PP equal exactly to the structure's Credit Value. Daily progress is generated based on the crafter's skill variance and the tier of their infrastructure. A creator's Daily Progress in Productivity Points is calculated by taking their Craft Check Result, subtracting 10, and multiplying that number by the tool's Tier Multiplier.  
> **Daily Progress (PP) \= (Craft Check Result \- 10\) \* Tool Tier Multiplier**  
*(See Section VIII, Step 5 for the comprehensive Tool Tier Multiplier table).*  
Under this engine, a Mastercraft Arcology Module (DC 30, Target PP \= 40,960) would take a hobbyist with Basic Tools (x10 multiplier) over 409 days to build. However, an advanced engineering team utilizing a Nanoforge (x1,000 multiplier) with a high check result can materialize the identical structure in less than two days.

**Macro-Scale Construction and Faction Labor Pools:**  
For astronomical projects like a Dreadnought Shipyard (DC 50, Value \~10.5 Million Cr) or a Titanic Arcology (DC 60+, Value \~167 Million Cr), individual crafting is physically impossible. Factions utilize Labor Pools to execute macro-scale construction. The Daily PP output is simply the sum of individual workers multiplied by their tools.  
A planetary shipyard employing 1,000 structural engineers operating Industrial (x200) fabrication tools can generate 1,000,000 PP per day, completing a massive capital installation in roughly 10.5 days. This macro-economic logic drives factional warfare; maintaining these construction yards requires an uninterrupted flow of massive liquid capital (5.25 Million Cr in raw materials per installation), forcing factions to fight for territorial resources to keep their production engines fed.
