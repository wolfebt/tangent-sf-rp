# **COMBAT**

**The combat system uses a 2d10 dice system that factors in character skills, abilities, and other modifiers. Combat actions are determined by skill level, with higher skill levels unlocking additional actions per turn.**  
**In the game, combat employs a 2d10 dice system, which adds an element of randomness and uncertainty to each encounter. The outcome of actions is determined by rolling two ten-sided dice (2d10) and adding relevant modifiers. These modifiers take into account character skills, abilities, and various situational factors.**

**Character skills play a significant role in combat. Each character has a unique set of skills that determine their combat capabilities. These skills include weapon proficiency, combat techniques, and special abilities. The skill level of a character influences the combat actions they can perform during their turn. For example, a character with high swordsmanship skill may be able to perform a flurry of attacks, while a character with high stealth skill may be able to hide from their opponents.**

**As characters progress and their skill levels increase, they unlock additional actions, enhancing their combat capabilities. This allows for more strategic and dynamic combat encounters. For instance, a character with a higher level of swordsmanship skill may be able to execute more powerful attacks or counter enemy attacks more effectively.**

**The 2d10 dice system introduces an element of chance into combat, ensuring that no two encounters are exactly the same. This can lead to exciting and unexpected outcomes, adding tension and excitement to every battle. The combination of character skills, abilities, modifiers, and random dice rolls creates a rich and engaging combat experience that keeps players engaged and on the edge of their seats.**

## **COMBAT ACTIONS**

A character may attack with as many actions as the used skill allows. They may also make as many **Active Defense** checks as allowed based on their Defense Skill Rank (all with a \-5 subsequent penalty after the first reaction).

### 

### **Standard Actions**

* **Attack:** Make a melee or ranged attack against a target.  
* **Active Defense:** Roll a Defense check to replace static Defense or negate specific maneuvers (Reaction).  
* **Aiming:** Spend an action to steady a shot. Gain **\+2 Strike** per round of Aiming (Max bonus \= 1/2 Skill Rank).  
* **Called Shot:** Attack a specific location (Limb/Head/Component). **\-5 Strike Penalty**. If successful, damage is applied directly to that location for the purpose of Disabling/Destroying it.  
* **Feint:** Make a **Bluff** check vs. **Insight** (Sense Motive). Success leads target into a False Defense, allowing a Sneak Attack at \-5 Strike (Target has NO Defense).

### 

### **Movement Actions**

* **Evasive Movement:** Moving quickly while defensive. Gain **\+1 Defense** base, plus **\+1 Defense per 10ft** of Movement Speed used.  
* **Subtle:** Moving quietly to avoid attention. Required for stealth-based Skill Actions.  
* **Braced:** Set to receive a charge or fire heavy weapons. Negates penalties for heavy recoil or grants Advantage against being tripped/shoved.

## **AUTOMATIC WEAPONS**

Automatic weaponry functions similarly to other weapons but with special considerations for volume of fire. All Range Penalties are   
**Doubled** during automatic fire.

* **Burst Fire (Short):** 3-round burst. Expends more ammo but grants **\+1 Strike**.  
* **Full Auto (Long):** 10+ rounds. **\-1 Recoil Penalty** per 10 rounds fired.  
  * *Effect:* For every point the Attack Roll exceeds the Defense DC, an **additional bullet hits** (up to total fired).  
  * *Damage:* Initial hit deals normal damage. Each extra hit deals **\+1d Damage** (only if the initial hit penetrated DR).  
* **Area Suppression (Blasting):**  
  * **General Area:** Targets take **\+2 Damage Dice** for every 5 points over DC 10\.  
  * **Focused Blast Zone:** Targets take **\+100% Damage Dice** for every 10 points over DC 15\.

## **DAMAGE & HEALTH**

### **Calculating Damage**

**Formula:** (Weapon Dice \+ Ability Mod \+ Precision/Skill Bonuses) \- (Target Armor DR \+ Target CON Mod) \= Total Damage

* **Precision Damage:** Bonuses from Skill Rank, Sneak Attacks, or Vital Strikes.  
* **Armor DR:** The value of the target's protective gear.  
* **Constitution (CON) Mod:** The target's natural physical resilience reduces damage taken.

### 

### **Damage Types**

Different weapons deal different damage types, which interact with armor and physiology differently.

* **Kinetic:** Physical impact. Subtypes include Blunt (Crushing), Slashing (Cutting), and Piercing (Puncturing).  
* **Force:** Kinetic subtype that **ignores 1/2 of Target's Armor DR**.  
* **Energy:**  
  * **Fire/Heat:** Burns organic matter.  
  * **Cold/Cryo:** Slows targets or shatters armor.  
  * **Sonic:** Ignores most physical armor.  
  * **Voltic (Electrical):** Stuns and damages electronics.  
  * **Corrosive (Acid):** Ongoing damage to armor and flesh.  
  * **Disruptor:** Breaks molecular bonds.  
* **Radiation (Ra-D):** Damages cellular structure. Effective against unshielded targets. Can cause sickness/mutation.  
* **Mental:** Direct trauma to the mind/nervous system. Often ignores Physical Armor entirely.

  * **Forearm**  Called Shot \-3

Specialized Locations

* **Tentacles / Wings / Fins**  Called Shot \-2 (full limb size), \-3 (Half-Limb size or thin) or \-4 (hand size or fibrous)

### **Limb Damage & Critical Injuries**

Specific body locations have their own damage thresholds.

* **Disabled:** If a limb takes significant damage (GM Discretion or Called Shot), it is useless until healed.  
* **Destroyed:** Massive damage to a limb may sever or mangle it beyond repair.  
* **Synth vs. Bio:** Synthetic limbs can take **50% more damage** before being Disabled/Destroyed than biological ones, but they may not get a Stamina check to remain functional at Disabled status.

## 

  **The Mortality State (0 Hit Points)**

  When a character's Health Points reach 0, they enter the Mortality State, which involves the following conditions:

* **Unconscious and Incapacitated:** The character immediately falls **Prone** and is **Incapacitated**.  
* **Bleeding Out:** At the beginning of the character's turn, they suffer 1 point of **Stability Damage**.  
* **Stability Threshold:** A character has a **Stability Points** total equal to their **Constitution Score \+ 5**.  
* **Death:** If a character's Stability Points are reduced to 0, they are considered **dead**.  
* **Stabilization and Recovery:** The character stops **Bleeding Out** and is stabilized if they receive any magical healing or a successful **Medicine Check** (DC 15\) is made to aid them.

### **How Scaling Works in Combat**

* **Attack Rolls:** Size doesn't directly affect attack rolls, but larger creatures might have reach advantages or penalties depending on their target's size.  
* **Damage:** Weapon dice are multiplied by the **Scaling Modifier**. No damage is reduced due to the target’s size as the target's structure is adjusted by the scaling.  
  * *Example:* A Huge creature (x5 Scaling) deals 5 times the base damage dice and its structure is 5x the baseline of a medium construct.  
* **Defense:** The **Combat Modifier** adjusts a creature's Defense score based on its size relative to its target.  
* **Distance:** Speed and ranges are also multiplied by the **Scaling Modifier**, allowing larger creatures to cover more ground.

# **EDGE**

# **EDGE**

*Edge refers to tactical advantage modifiers that characters can gain or lose based on their movements, positions, and the environment during combat. These modifiers can significantly influence the outcome of attacks and defense, making them a crucial aspect of tactical combat. Here's a breakdown of some of the Edge modifiers:*

## 

## **Aiming**

Having the target in sights and time to steady and lead the shot, gain \+2 to Strike per round of Aiming (up to 1/2 the attacker’s Skill Level)

## **Flanking**

Advantage gained when an attacker has allies positioned on multiple sides of the target. This positioning creates an opportunity to exploit openings in the target's defenses, granting a \+2 bonus to hit.

## **Advancing Target**

Target approaching without Cover or Evasion. Extra Attack on target at \-5 to Strike and re-roll Initiative if successfully moved into Melee.

## **Retreating Target**

Target quickly leaving the area forgoing any evasion or cover, Jog/Run/Sprint Speed. Extra attack on target at \-5 to Strike and reroll Initiative.

## **High Ground**

\+2 to Strike and \+2 to Critical Damage Range

## **Prone**

On the ground   
\- vs an opponent in Melee/Point Blank Range they will have High Ground Bonus  
\- vs a Ranged opponent receive a \+2 Defense Bonus for each Range Category after Point Blank for ground cover   
  (Opponent with High Ground at Range will reduce bonuses based on line of effect accordingly)   
\- with a successful Defense check the Prone Combatant may be able to take full cover from Ranged Attacks

## **Braced**

Set to receive a Charge or to stay in place. Block usable as a counter attack on getting hit (or readied weapon) and bonus is gained for Advancing Target

## **Charge**

on successful strike add \+1d to Attack Damage and \-1 to strike per stage of speed (walk, jog, run, sprint), also an additional \+1 Point of Impact Damage per 10 ft of Speed (up to a maximum of \+100% increase in the attack’s damage)

## **Close In**

Guarded approach, defensive advancement at base speed. NO Modifiers and not counted as an Advancing Target

## **Withdraw**

Pulling back, defensively at base speed. NO Modifiers and not counted as a Retreating Target

## **Evasive Movement**

Fully Defensive while moving quickly, \+1 Defense and another \+1 per 10ft Movement Speed

## **Subtle**

Condition which is for quiet, sneaky or low key actions trying to not draw attention. Used along with other conditions listed but required for availability of different Skill Actions.

## **Feint**

An Attacker can make a Bluff check (vs Sense Motive) to lead the Target into a False Defense, forcing an opening for a Sneak Attack made at \-5 to Strike (with the Target having NO Defense)

## **Surprise / Sneak Attack**

The Target is unaware of an incoming Attack and will be unable to make any Defense check to Evade the Attack and subsequently reduce damage if not Evaded.  
If an Attacker is able to catch a Target unaware they will also have the opportunity to inflict more damage or other effects if so trained.  
This is the “Getting the drop on em’”, whether it’s from a literal backstab, sniper’s shot, waiting in ambush, to firing a pistol thru a pocket or casually stabbing someone in conversation \- the attack went unnoticed until it happened with no defense allowed. Often due to Stealth (vs Insight) but may also be gained from a Bluff (vs Sense Motive) and / or situational/environmental factors.   
Made at \-5 to hit but with NO DEFENSE from the target, Damage is made at Advantage in addition to other effects.  
*Danger Sense Feature will allow an Advantageous check to be aware of an impending attack regardless of the source*

# **DAMAGE**

# **DAMAGE**

*The attacker rolls 2d10, adds their relevant Ability Score modifier and any applicable Skill modifiers, and compares the total to the target's Defense. If the total equals or exceeds the target's Defense, the attack hits.*  
*Upon a successful hit, the attacker then rolls their weapon's damage dice, adds any relevant damage modifiers, and resolves defense soak. Armor Damage Reduction (DR) reduces damage first. Any remaining damage that penetrates armor is then reduced by the character's natural Stamina DR, down to a minimum of 1 point (if armor absorbs the entire blow, damage is 0). The resulting number is the damage applied to the target's pool.*  
*Different weapons deal different damage types, which can be more or less effective against certain targets. For example, piercing damage is good against unarmored targets, while blunt damage is better against armored targets.*

### **Lethal vs. Non-Lethal Damage & Pools**
* **Lethal Damage:** Applied directly to **Health**.
* **Non-Lethal Damage:** Applied to **Vitality**. If non-lethal damage exceeds the character's remaining Vitality, the excess spillover damage becomes **lethal** and is deducted from **Health**.
* **Synthetics & Constructs:** Use a combined **Structure** pool ($\text{Structure} = \text{Health} + \text{Vitality}$). Synthetics and constructs are completely **immune to non-lethal damage**.
* **Base Pools:** Both Vitality and Health have a base score of 30, increased by 5 points per CP spent up to a maximum increase of $5 \times \text{Stamina}$.

Core Mechanic  
Damage is calculated by summing several components:  
**Weapon Die Damage**  
Determined by the weapon used and its inherent damage capabilities.

**Relevant Ability**  
The character's strength, agility, or other relevant ability modifier is added, reflecting their physical prowess.

**Precision Damage**  
This includes bonuses from skill level and additional effects like sneak attacks, rewarding accuracy and tactical positioning.

**Target's Armor DR**  
The target's Armor Damage Reduction (DR) is subtracted first, representing their armor's effectiveness in mitigating incoming kinetic or energetic trauma.

**Stamina Natural DR**  
All characters possess a natural DR equal to their **Stamina score**. Any damage that penetrates external defenses/armor is reduced by Stamina DR down to a **minimum of 1 point**. If external armor completely deflects the blow, 0 damage penetrates.

Damage Types  
Tangent features a variety of damage types, including kinetic, energy, and other special types. Kinetic damage encompasses blunt, slashing, and piercing attacks, while energy damage includes fire, cold, and other elemental or exotic forms.

Armor and Penetration  
Armor plays a crucial role in reducing damage, with different armor types offering specific DR against different damage types. Some weapons, however, have penetration effects that allow them to bypass a portion of the target's DR.

Critical Hits and Fumbles  
A natural 20 (rolling double 10s on 2d10) on the attack roll results in a critical hit, increasing damage (doubling weapon dice and adding tactical potency). **Critical hits do not automatically make non-lethal attacks lethal**; a non-lethal critical hit inflicts critical non-lethal damage to Vitality (with only excess spilling to Health). Conversely, a natural 2 (rolling double 1s) results in a critical failure/fumble, subtracting from the attack score and potentially causing negative situational consequences.

Location-Based Effects  
Attacks can target specific body locations, with successful hits potentially causing debilitating effects like being winded, disarmed, or hobbled. Critical hits can even cause more severe consequences, such as unconsciousness or crippled limbs.

Healing and Recovery  
Characters can recover from damage through rest, medical attention, or the use of metaphysical abilities. However, severe injuries may require extended recovery periods or even cause permanent disabilities.

Additional Notes  
The damage system interacts with other mechanics, such as cover, movement, and tactical positioning, adding a layer of strategic depth to combat encounters. Furthermore, the system allows for customization through weapon enhancements, special abilities, and other factors, enabling players to tailor their characters' combat capabilities to their preferred playstyle.

# **DAMAGE TYPES**

*Tangent features a variety of damage types, primarily kinetic and energy-based. Kinetic damage includes blunt, slashing, and piercing attacks, with force damage as a subtype that ignores half of the target's Armor DR. Energy damage encompasses fire, cold, phased, sonic, voltic, corrosive, radiation, and disruptor damage, each with unique effects like burning, slowing, bypassing armor, or stunning. Armor provides Damage Resist (DR) against specific damage types, and some weapons have penetrating effects or ammunition to bypass DR.*

## **ENERGY**

### **Pyro / Fire Damage**

This damage type focuses on burning and incinerating targets, causing immediate pain and potential long-term damage. It's particularly effective against flammable materials and creatures vulnerable to heat. Fire damage often inflicts ongoing burning damage, and critical hits can ignite the target, causing additional damage over time.  
Metaphysical

* Pyrokinesis: The ability to create and control fire with the mind. This includes conjuring flames, shaping them into various forms, and directing their intensity. Pyrokinesis can be used for both offensive and utility purposes, such as creating a wall of fire or igniting a distant target.  
* Fire Evocation: Summoning fire elementals or spirits to fight on your behalf. These creatures can unleash powerful fire attacks, are often resistant or immune to fire damage, and can provide tactical advantages in combat.  
* Fire Enchantments: Imbuing weapons or objects with fire magic. This can grant them the ability to ignite targets on contact, deal additional fire damage, or even provide protection against fire-based attacks.

Archaic Tech

* Torches, Arrows, and Grenades: The simplest forms of fire weaponry. Torches can be used for melee attacks, arrows tipped with flammable substances for ranged attacks, and grenades filled with incendiary materials for area-effect damage.  
* Flamethrowers: Primitive flamethrowers project a stream of ignited fuel, dealing continuous fire damage to targets in a cone or line. They are effective at close range and can cause widespread panic.  
* Fire Traps: Mechanisms that use fire to damage or deter intruders. These can range from simple pits filled with flammable liquids to complex traps that ignite automatically when triggered.

Modern Tech

* Flamethrowers: Modern flamethrowers are more advanced, with greater range, accuracy, and fuel efficiency. They can be mounted on vehicles or wielded by infantry, providing a powerful area denial weapon.  
* Incendiary Rounds: Ammunition for firearms can be designed to ignite upon impact, setting targets ablaze and causing ongoing fire damage. This is especially effective against flammable materials or creatures vulnerable to fire.  
* Fire Bombs: Explosive devices filled with incendiary compounds can be deployed to create large fires, damaging structures and causing widespread damage in an area.

Advanced Tech

* Laser Weapons: High-energy laser beams can be focused to ignite targets, causing both immediate and ongoing fire damage. Laser weapons are known for their precision and range, making them effective at targeting specific body parts or vulnerable areas.  
* Plasma Weapons: These weapons project superheated plasma, dealing devastating fire damage and potentially melting through armor. Plasma weapons are powerful but often have shorter ranges and may require cooling periods between shots.  
* Energy Pulse Weapons: Advanced energy weapons can emit pulses of intense heat, setting targets on fire and causing damage over time. These weapons can have various firing modes, including burst and spray, allowing for tactical flexibility.

Additional Notes: Fire damage can cause ongoing burning damage, and some weapons may have additional effects like armor-piercing or increased critical hit chance against flammable targets. Characters can use fire weapons to target vulnerable areas for increased damage or to create environmental hazards that can hinder or damage opponents.

### **Cryo / Cold Damage**

This damage type focuses on freezing and chilling targets, causing immediate pain, slowing movement, and potentially causing long-term damage like frostbite or hypothermia. It's particularly effective against creatures vulnerable to cold and can be used to create hazardous environmental conditions. # **VEHICULAR COMBAT**

## 

## **I. OPERATIONAL FUNDAMENTALS**

In the Tangent universe, vehicular combat is not a separate game; it is an escalation of the core 2d10 mechanics. Whether piloting a nimble hover-bike or commanding a gargantuan void-cruiser, the loop remains: **Action, Reaction, Consequence.**

### 

### **1\. The Tactical Round**

Vehicular combat operates on the standard 6-second Combat Round, but the *Scale* of the action changes.

* **Initiative:** Determined by the **Pilot**.  
  * *Formula:* 2d10 \+ Agility Mod \+ Alertness \+ Handling Modifier  
  * *Handling Modifier:* A stat intrinsic to the vehicle chassis (e.g., a Fighter Jet has \+4, a Capital Ship has \-4).  
* **The Crew Turn:** Unlike personal combat where one character acts, a vehicle is a platform for *multiple* actors.  
  * **Pilot:** Controls Movement, Evasion (Defense), and Fixed Forward Weapons.  
  * **Gunner(s):** Control Turrets and Pintle-mounted weapons. They act on the Pilot's Initiative count but track their own Actions.  
  * **Engineer/System Op:** Manages Shields, Sensors, and Repairs.

### 

### **2\. Scale & The Size Modifier**

The most critical factor in vehicular combat is **Scale**. A pistol cannot damage a tank, and a battleship’s main battery cannot track a single infantryman.  
Targeting Modifiers (Size Difference):  
When an attacker targets a vehicle, or a vehicle targets a smaller entity, apply the Combat Modifiers from 01.01.09 Scaling.

* **David vs. Goliath:** Small targets shooting Large targets get a bonus to Hit (it’s hard to miss a barn), but likely fail to penetrate DR.  
* **Goliath vs. David:** Large weapons suffer penalties to Hit smaller, nimble targets (Tracking Penalty).

## 

## 

## **II. DOMAINS OF WAR**

### **1\. Land (Terrestrial & Walker)**

Combat defined by **Terrain** and **Cover**.

* **Cover:** Vehicles can use buildings and terrain for Cover (Defense Bonus \+4 to \+10). A Hull Down position (only turret visible) grants \+4 Defense.  
* **Ramming:** A desperate maneuver.  
  * *Attack:* Pilot makes a melee attack roll using Pilot Skill.  
  * *Damage:* 1d10 per 10ft of Speed. Both vehicles take this damage. Heavier vehicle takes half.  
* **Walker Mechanics:** Bipedal/Quadruped mecha can **Stomp** infantry (Standard Melee Attack) and step over obstacles that would block wheeled vehicles.

### 

### **2\. Nautical (Surface & Submerged)**

Combat defined by **Depth** and **Detection**.

* **Sonar/Sensors:** Visual range is often zero. Combat relies on *System Ops (Sensors)* checks to acquire a Target Lock. Without a Lock, attacks are made at Disadvantage.  
* **Depth Bands:**  
  * *Surface:* Standard operation.  
  * *Periscope Depth:* Concealment vs Surface.  
  * *Deep:* High pressure. Hull breaches here are catastrophic (see Damage).

### 

### **3\. Atmospheric (Aerial)**

Combat defined by **Altitude** and **Energy Management**.

* **The Dogfight (Pilot vs. Pilot):** When two aircraft engage, they enter a Dogfight.  
  * *Mechanic:* Opposed Pilot checks at the start of the round.  
  * *Winner:* Gains **Advantage** on attack rolls this turn and chooses positioning (Tail/Flank).  
  * *Loser:* Is considered Flat-Footed against the Winner.  
* **Stalling:** Flying slower than the vehicle's Stall Speed forces a Pilot check (DC 20\) or the craft begins to fall.

### 

### **4\. Interstellar (Space)**

Combat defined by **Vectors**, **Heat**, and **3D Distance**.

* **Newtonian Drift:** In zero-G, a vehicle continues moving in its last direction unless thrust is applied. A Pilot can cut engines to drift silently (Stealth) while retaining speed.  
* **Heat Signatures:** Firing energy weapons or using main thrusters flares the vehicle's signature.  
  * *Mechanic:* Passive Sensors detect flared ships automatically. Active Sensors (Ping) are required to Lock On to "Cold" ships.  
* **Proximity Damage:** (As per *Scaling*) Capital ship weapons are so massive that near-misses still cause concussive damage to fighter-scale craft.

## 

## 

## **III. COMBAT INTERACTIONS**

### **1\. Pilot vs. Pilot (Vehicle vs. Vehicle)**

The duel of machines.

* **Evasive Maneuvers (Action):** The Pilot trades their Attack action to impose Disadvantage on all incoming fire.  
* **Jinking:** A Reaction to being fired upon. Pilot rolls Pilot Skill vs. Attack Roll. Success negates the hit but costs movement on the next turn.  
* **Fixed vs. Turret:**  
  * *Fixed Weapons:* Fire in a 45-degree arc from the front. Require the Pilot to point the vehicle at the target.  
  * *Turrets:* Fire 360-degrees. Operated by Gunners independently of movement.

### 

### **2\. Gunner vs. Mecha (Infantry vs. Vehicle)**

The asymmetry of scale.

* **The Armor Threshold:** Most vehicles have high **Damage Resistance (DR)**. Small arms (pistols, rifles) often cannot penetrate.  
  * *Rule:* If weapon Damage \< DR, the shot bounces. No effect.  
* **Called Shots (Weak Points):** Infantry can bypass heavy armor by targeting sensors, tracks, or exhaust ports.  
  * *Mechanic:* Attack at \-5 penalty. On hit, ignore 50% of DR or disable a specific system (e.g., "Immobilized").  
* **Boarding:** A daring infantry unit can climb a slow/stopped vehicle.  
  * *Mechanic:* Athletics check vs. Vehicle Speed/Defense. Success places the unit *on* the hull, bypassing Scale penalties for subsequent attacks.

### 

### **3\. Gunner vs. Structure (Siege Warfare)**

Demolition and breaching.

* **Static Defense:** Structures have **0 Agility** and effective Defense of 5\. They are auto-hit by most attacks.  
* **Hardness (DR) & Modules:** Buildings are sections of **Modules** (e.g., a 10x10 wall section).  
  * *Siege Damage:* Heavy Weapons (Explosive, Energy) deal **Double Damage** to stationary structures.  
* **Breaching:** Reducing a Module to 0 SP creates a Breach (hole) large enough for infantry. Reducing it to \-50% SP causes **Collapse** of that section and overhead floors.

## 

## 

## **IV. DAMAGE & CATASTROPHE**

Vehicles do not just die; they degrade, burn, and explode.

### 

### **1\. Damage Thresholds**

* **Glancing Blow:** Damage does not exceed DR. No effect (ping\!).  
* **Penetrating Hit:** Damage exceeds DR. Subtract DR from Damage, apply remainder to **Structure Points (SP)**.  
* **Critical Hit:** Natural 20 or exceeding Defense by 10+. Roll on the **System Failure Table**.

### 

### **2\. System Failure Table (d6)**

When a vehicle takes a Critical Hit or reaches 50% SP:

1. **Motive System:** Speed reduced by 50%. (Treads thrown, wing clipped).  
2. **Weapon System:** One random weapon disabled/jammed.  
3. **Sensor/Comms:** Blinded or silenced. Disadvantage on targeting.  
4. **Crew Compartment:** Pilot/Crew takes half the damage dealt to the vehicle.  
5. **Power Plant:** Vehicle creates smoke/fire. Takes 1d6 Burn damage per turn.  
6. **Catastrophic Leak:** Fuel/Reactor unstable. Explodes in 1d4 rounds unless repaired.

### 

### **3\. Destruction**

* **0 SP (Disabled):** Vehicle stops. Systems offline. Can be repaired.  
* **Negative SP \> 50% Max (Destroyed):** The vehicle is a wreck. Scrap value only.  
* **Catastrophic Explosion:** If a vehicle is destroyed by Fire or Explosive damage, it detonates.  
  * *Radius:* 10ft per Size Category.  
  * *Damage:* 1d6 per 10 SP of the vehicle's max structure.
